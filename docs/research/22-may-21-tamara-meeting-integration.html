May 21 Tamara Meeting Integration

Plain read

The May 21 meeting did not change the plan. It made the plan easier to explain.

The path is:

  1. Make the current product reliable enough to charge for.
  2. Keep the first use case narrow: saved-contact family coordination.
  3. Add voice after the text/email loop works.
  4. Add camp and activity automation later, after the first paid proof.
  5. Put ownership, IP, cost recovery, and decision rights in writing before deeper work starts.

Executive takeaways

The core product promise should stay simple:

Looheru gets the task done and closes the loop.

That is stronger than "AI helps with tasks." Parents do not need another dashboard. They need fewer open loops.

The strongest product path is still a sequence of tranches:

  1. Tranche 1: deterministic saved-contact coordination, onboarding, payment, plan caps, cost tracking, admin visibility, and paid pilot readiness.
  2. Tranche 2: voice logistics, starting with parent voice intake and then carefully scoped scheduling calls.
  3. Tranche 3: camp and activity registration automation, starting narrow by geography and season.

What changed in the roadmap

Tranche 1: make the current loop chargeable

The first build should stay narrow.

Build around contacts the parent already trusts. Do not ask an agent to mine contacts, texts, or email automatically in the first version.

For the first sitter use case, most families may only need a few trusted contacts. That makes the product easier to launch and easier to trust.

Tranche 1 should include:

  • manual trusted-contact onboarding,
  • deterministic SMS/email outreach,
  • one clear parent request at a time,
  • simple payment,
  • plan caps,
  • task-level cost tracking,
  • admin visibility,
  • pilot reporting,
  • privacy and retention rules,
  • refund or extension rules for failed in-scope tasks.

Project update: describe the first 4-week build as hardening and pilot readiness. Do not describe it as building from zero.

Tranche 2: add voice after the first loop works

Voice should move sooner in the roadmap, but it should not lead the first pilot.

Use voice where it helps complete a real scheduling task. Keep the category as family logistics. Do not market Looheru as health tech or clinical AI.

Voice planning should include:

  • parent voice intake,
  • provider or business call scripts,
  • a low-risk call policy,
  • clear disclosure rules,
  • a structured result payload back to the core system,
  • a quality bar for trust, latency, handoffs, and failed calls.

Project update: plan voice during tranche 1. Build and test it as a separate tranche after the first paid loop is stable.

Tranche 3: test camp and activity automation later

Camp automation is still a strong idea. It should not distract from the first paid proof.

The best first model is parent-side automation, not a camp marketplace.

The system can later:

  • gather child ages, dates, preferences, and location,
  • find local options,
  • group options by week,
  • ask the parent to approve choices,
  • submit registrations where the site allows it,
  • track whether registration completed.

Project update: treat this as a later browser/web automation lane. Start with one geography and one seasonal cycle.

  • first revenue pays direct costs and approved build-cost recovery before profit or revenue split.

Strategic framing update

Do not frame Looheru as only special-needs support or as health tech.

The better category is:

family task execution for parents carrying too many open loops.

The story can still include lived experience and high-need family complexity. But the market should stay broader:

  • busy parents,
  • physician moms and high-performing working parents,
  • medically complex or special-needs families as a high-need beachhead,
  • families that need real task execution, not another reminder system.
