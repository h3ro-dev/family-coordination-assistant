Meeting purpose

The purpose of the May 21 meeting was to review the Looheru opportunity with Tamara and decide what had to be clearer before a partnership decision.

The meeting focused on:

  • the current product and prototype,
  • the first paid use case,
  • the A/B/C partnership options,
  • ownership and IP,
  • voice and camp automation timing,
  • the next decision checkpoint.

Meeting outcomes

The meeting produced these working outcomes:

  1. Keep Options A, B, and C in the proposal.
  2. Remove recommendation language that makes one option feel forced.
  3. Make Option C clearer: at-cost build, 35%-40% ownership, first revenue pays costs, then revenue split.
  4. Keep Tamara's founder control explicit.
  5. Make company-owned IP and background-IP carveouts explicit.
  6. Use a 4-week tranche-one build/test sprint as the first external promise.
  7. Move voice into tranche 2, with planning during tranche 1.
  8. Move camp/activity automation into tranche 3.
  9. Treat May 29 as the decision checkpoint.

Tranche updates

Tranche 0: decision and agreement prep

Timing: now through May 29.

Purpose: make the decision easy.

Work:

  • finalize the A/B/C option comparison,
  • define IP and ownership terms,
  • define founder-control terms,
  • define Option C cost recovery,
  • confirm tranche 1 resources,
  • confirm legal review path,
  • confirm meeting attendees.

Decision at end: does Tamara proceed, and under which option?

Tranche 1: paid coordination loop

Target: 4-week AI-native build and test sprint.

Purpose: make the existing saved-contact loop reliable enough to charge for.

Build:

  • manual trusted-contact onboarding,
  • deterministic SMS/email outreach,
  • one active parent request at a time,
  • simple payment,
  • plan caps,
  • task-level cost tracking,
  • admin visibility,
  • pilot reporting,
  • privacy and retention rules,
  • refund or extension rules for failed in-scope tasks.

Test:

  • can a parent make a request quickly,
  • can Looheru contact trusted people,
  • can the system return clear options,
  • can the parent choose one,
  • can the system close the loop,
  • can the business see cost and task success.

Decision at end: is tranche 1 strong enough for a paid pilot?

Tranche 2: voice logistics

Timing: plan during tranche 1; build after tranche 1 is stable.

Purpose: use voice where it helps complete family logistics.

Build or test:

  • parent voice intake,
  • scripted provider or business calls,
  • call disclosure rules,
  • acceptable call-type list,
  • structured result payload back to the core system,
  • quality checks for trust, latency, accuracy, and failed calls.

Boundary:

  • Keep the product positioned as family logistics.
  • Do not market it as clinical AI.
  • Do not let voice destabilize the core coordination loop.

Decision at end: does voice improve task completion enough to expand?

Tranche 3: camp and activity automation

Timing: after the first paid loop and voice path are better understood.

Purpose: reduce the parent burden around camps, activities, and seasonal planning.

Build or test:

  • collect child ages, dates, preferences, and location,
  • find local options,
  • group options by week,
  • ask the parent to approve choices,
  • submit registrations where the website allows it,
  • track whether registration completed.

Boundary:

  • Start with one geography.
  • Start with one seasonal cycle.
  • Do not turn the first product into a camp marketplace.

Decision at end: can parent-side automation create enough value without building a two-sided marketplace?

What should not change

These points should stay stable unless the team makes a deliberate decision to change them:

  • The first product is task execution, not another planning app.
  • The first use case is saved-contact coordination.
  • The first pilot should prove payment and task completion.
  • Voice is important, but it is not tranche 1.
  • Camps and activities are valuable, but they are not tranche 1.
  • Tamara keeps founder decision leadership.
  • Option C requires much clearer economics than a normal services agreement.
