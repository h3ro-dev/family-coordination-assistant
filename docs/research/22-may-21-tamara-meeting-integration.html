Tranche 1: paid coordination loop

Purpose: make the current saved-contact coordination loop reliable enough to charge for.

Build:

  • manual trusted-contact onboarding,
  • deterministic SMS/email outreach,
  • one active parent request at a time,
  • simple payment,
  • plan caps,
  • task-level cost tracking,
  • admin visibility,
  • pilot reporting,
  • privacy and retention rules,
  • refund or extension rules for failed in-scope tasks.

Test:

  • Can a parent make a request quickly?
  • Can Looheru contact trusted people?
  • Can the system return clear options?
  • Can the parent choose one?
  • Can the system close the loop?
  • Can the team see cost, completion, and failure data?

Decision at end:

  • Is tranche 1 strong enough for a paid pilot?

Tranche 2: voice logistics

Purpose: use voice where it helps complete family logistics.

Plan during tranche 1. Build after tranche 1 is stable.

Build or test:

  • parent voice intake,
  • scripted provider or business calls,
  • call disclosure rules,
  • acceptable call-type list,
  • structured result payload back to the core system,
  • quality checks for trust, latency, accuracy, and failed calls.

Boundary:

  • Keep the product positioned as family logistics.
  • Do not market it as clinical AI.
  • Do not let voice destabilize the core coordination loop.

Decision at end:

  • Does voice improve task completion enough to expand?

Tranche 3: camp and activity automation

Purpose: reduce parent burden around camps, activities, and seasonal planning.

Build or test:

  • child age, schedule, preference, and location intake,
  • local option discovery,
  • week-by-week shortlists,
  • parent approval,
  • registration submission where the site allows it,
  • booking status tracking.

Boundary:

  • Start with one geography.
  • Start with one seasonal cycle.
  • Do not turn the first product into a camp marketplace.

Decision at end:

  • Can parent-side automation create enough value without building a two-sided marketplace?

Duckbill takeaways

Duckbill is useful because it validates a willingness to pay for done-for-you life admin.

Current Duckbill signals from its public pricing page:

  • Essentials: $49/month after first month, about 3-5 tasks per month.
  • Individual: $99/month after first month, about 4-6 tasks per month.
  • Household: $169/month after first month, about 8-12 tasks per month.
  • Household Plus: $350/month after first month, about 16-20 tasks per month.
  • It uses background-checked human specialists plus AI.
  • Members submit tasks by app, text, or email.
  • It includes calls on the user's behalf.
  • Family examples include daycare paperwork, prescription refills, pediatrician scheduling, birthday venue booking, nanny hours, and dentist scheduling.

What Looheru should learn:

  • A task-bandwidth model may be easier to understand than pure "AI subscription" pricing.
  • Completed tasks are more valuable than reminders or dashboards.
  • Family use cases are already legible to the market.
  • Duckbill's broad assistant model creates space for Looheru to be narrower: family coordination first.
  • Duckbill relies on human specialists. Looheru's strategic bet is a more autonomous coordination layer, starting with trusted contacts.

Source: Duckbill pricing and task examples.

Legal facilitation notes

These are prep notes for counsel. They are not final legal advice.

QuestionWorking answer for planningMay 29 action
Can Looheru use AI voice for outbound calls?Yes, but outbound AI voice calls create TCPA risk. The safest early path is parent-requested calls, clear identity, clear AI disclosure, no sales pitch, and call-type limits.Ask counsel to review call scripts, consent language, and disclosure.
Does AI need to disclose itself in Utah?Utah's AI law requires disclosure if asked in ordinary consumer interactions, and start-of-interaction disclosure for regulated services. Looheru should disclose AI clearly as a product rule.Add default AI disclosure to voice and text policies.
Is this telemarketing?The early product should not call third parties to sell them anything. It should call only to complete parent-requested logistics. That lowers telemarketing risk but does not remove TCPA/state-law review.Keep all early calls non-sales and parent-authorized.
Does HIPAA apply?Looheru is not automatically a covered entity just because it helps a parent schedule. Risk rises if it receives protected health information from providers, partners with providers, or handles health data at scale.Keep tranche 1 away from PHI-heavy workflows. Review any provider-facing workflow before launch.
Can calls be recorded or transcribed?Recording/transcription can trigger state consent rules. Treat recording as off by default unless counsel approves the notice flow.Decide whether recordings are needed at all.
What data should be collected?Collect the minimum needed to complete the task. Avoid sensitive health, custody, legal, and emergency categories in tranche 1.Add data boundaries to onboarding and task intake.
