The partnership in one sentence

Utlyze / SolutionStream would help turn Looheru from a working prototype into a company-owned, paid, measurable family coordination product, while Tamara stays the founder, brand, domain expert, and final product decision-maker.

Plain English:

  • Tamara owns the company vision.
  • The company owns company-created product IP, accounts, data, and customer relationships.
  • Utlyze / SolutionStream owns its background IP and general tools, with any required license documented.
  • The deeper the partnership, the more operating risk and upside Utlyze / SolutionStream takes.

The 12-18 week production path

The cleanest way to explain the roadmap is three production tranches. Each tranche should produce a decision.

Tranche 0 is agreement prep. It is not one of the three production tranches.

Tranche 0: agreement and ownership prep

Timing: before production work starts.

Purpose: remove avoidable future friction.

Outputs:

  • selected Option A, B, or C,
  • signed scope or operating agreement,
  • company/IP/account ownership checklist,
  • background IP schedule,
  • contributor and invention assignment plan,
  • source repo and deployment access map,
  • counsel review list,
  • first-tranche success criteria.

Decision:

  • Are we allowed to start tranche one?

Tranche 1: paid MVP coordination loop

Timing: usually weeks 1-4, with a 4-6 week external commitment.

Purpose: make the current saved-contact coordination loop reliable enough to charge for.

Build:

  • current repo audit and production hardening,
  • manual trusted-contact onboarding,
  • deterministic SMS/email coordination,
  • parent request intake,
  • one active request loop at a time,
  • simple payment or paid-pilot collection path,
  • plan caps and task caps,
  • task-level cost ledger,
  • admin dashboard for task status and failures,
  • privacy and retention rules,
  • refund or extension rule for failed in-scope tasks,
  • pilot reporting.

Test:

  • Can a parent set up without staff help?
  • Can the parent add trusted contacts?
  • Can the system contact those trusted people?
  • Can it handle no replies, unclear replies, opt-outs, duplicates, and cost caps?
  • Can the parent choose from clear options?
  • Can the system close the loop?
  • Can we see task cost, completion, failure reason, and repeat use?

First MVP deliverable:

  • a chargeable coordination product for a small pilot group,
  • a working setup flow,
  • trusted-contact coordination,
  • payment or paid-pilot intake,
  • task status visibility,
  • cost and completion reporting,
  • a clear decision packet for whether to scale, narrow, or pause.

Decision:

  • Is the first wedge strong enough for paid users?
  • Does the product save enough parent time to justify the price?
  • What must be fixed before voice is added?

Tranche 2: voice logistics

Timing: usually weeks 5-10 or 7-12, depending on tranche one stability.

Purpose: use voice only where it helps complete family logistics.

Build:

  • voice provider selection memo,
  • parent-facing voice intake,
  • structured task-to-call tool contract,
  • approved call-type list,
  • call disclosure script,
  • consent and opt-out rules,
  • parent-authorized outbound call workflow,
  • structured call-result payload back into the core system,
  • QA review for latency, accuracy, failed calls, and user trust.

Test:

  • Does voice make parent intake easier?
  • Can the system place scoped logistics calls without creating clinical, sales, or privacy risk?
  • Can call outcomes be summarized without storing more data than needed?
  • Can failed calls route back into the task system?
  • Does voice improve completion rate enough to justify added cost and risk?

Boundary:

  • Keep this as family logistics.
  • Do not market it as clinical AI.
  • Do not collect sensitive health details unless counsel approves the workflow.
  • Do not let voice destabilize the tranche one loop.

Decision:

  • Should voice expand, stay parent-facing only, or wait?

Tranche 3: camp and activity automation

Timing: usually weeks 11-18, or later if seasonal timing makes a later test smarter.

Purpose: reduce parent burden around camps, activities, and seasonal planning.

Build:

  • one-geography camp/activity inventory,
  • child age, schedule, location, and preference intake,
  • week-by-week fit grid,
  • parent approval flow,
  • registration attempt where the site supports it,
  • completion listener and status tracking,
  • failure handling when websites block automation or require manual review.

Test:

  • Can the system find enough local options to be useful?
  • Can it avoid becoming a camp marketplace?
  • Can the parent approve recommendations quickly?
  • Can registration be completed or prepared with less parent work?
  • Which parts should be autonomous, semi-autonomous, or not automated?

Boundary:

  • Start with one geography.
  • Start with one seasonal cycle.
  • Do not make Looheru a two-sided camp platform in this tranche.

Decision:

  • Is camp/activity automation valuable enough to become a major product line?

Option A: cash services

Plain read: Tamara pays market cash. Looheru keeps all equity.

What it means:

  • Utlyze / SolutionStream acts as a vendor and delivery partner.
  • Each tranche is separately scoped or approved.
  • No equity is issued.
  • Tamara keeps the cleanest ownership path.

What we cover:

  • Tranche 1 build and hardening if authorized.
  • Tranche 2 and 3 only if separately scoped.
  • Technical documentation, acceptance criteria, and handoff materials for paid work.

What this does not include by default:

  • cofounder-level responsibility,
  • long-term operating ownership,
  • fundraising support,
  • ongoing growth ownership,
  • unfunded later tranches.

Best fit:

  • Tamara wants maximum control and wants to buy execution with cash.

Option B: strategic build partner

Plain read: Tamara pays less cash now, and Utlyze / SolutionStream receives 10%-15% equity or warrants tied to real build and operating responsibility.

What it means:

  • We are more than a vendor.
  • We help own product execution, architecture, roadmap decisions, pilot evidence, and tranche planning.
  • Equity should vest through milestones over time, not appear as vague upside.

What we cover:

  • Everything in Option A for tranche one,
  • product ownership support,
  • technical roadmap,
  • vendor and architecture memos,
  • release cadence,
  • pilot review,
  • tranche two voice decisioning,
  • tranche three planning if the earlier tranches justify it.

Suggested equity logic:

  • total partner upside target: 10%-15%,
  • vesting over 24-36 months or explicit milestones,
  • tranche one vesting only after production-ready paid-pilot delivery,
  • later vesting tied to voice, camp automation, sponsor readiness, or agreed operating milestones,
  • unvested equity stops if the relationship ends.

Best fit:

  • Tamara wants a real build partner but does not want to make Utlyze / SolutionStream a cofounder-level owner.

Option C: studio / cofounder build

Plain read: Utlyze / SolutionStream builds at cost and becomes a 35%-40% company-building partner.

What it means:

  • This is not a discounted vendor project.
  • It is a company-building relationship.
  • We carry more operating risk and accept cost recovery before upside.
  • The agreement must be much clearer because the relationship is deeper.

What we cover:

  • the tranche one paid MVP build,
  • voice logistics path if tranche one works,
  • camp/activity automation path if tranche two and business timing support it,
  • ongoing product and technical leadership,
  • recurring engineering capacity,
  • vendor selection,
  • operating dashboard and evidence,
  • sponsor or cohort support if the pilot is strong,
  • hiring/vendor roadmap when the company scales.

Cost and revenue logic:

  • Utlyze / SolutionStream builds at actual cost, not market margin.
  • Cost lines should be transparent: people, vendors, telecom, model/API usage, hosting, tooling, and approved external spend.
  • First revenue should cover direct COGS.
  • After COGS, first revenue should repay approved build-cost recovery.
  • After recovery, revenue split should follow the operating agreement.

Ownership logic:

  • target ownership: 35%-40% for the company-building role,
  • Tamara remains founder and should keep founder decision control unless later documents change that,
  • company-created IP belongs to the company,
  • background IP is listed and licensed as needed,
  • reserved decisions, vesting, repurchase rights, and offboarding terms must be written before production work starts.

Best fit:

  • Tamara wants a true company-building partner and wants to conserve cash while giving up meaningful ownership.

Recommended way to articulate the decision

Do not frame this as "pick our favorite option."

Frame it this way:

The right option depends on how much responsibility you want us to carry. Option A is clean vendor execution. Option B is a strategic build partnership with some shared upside. Option C is the company-building path, where we build at cost and earn a much larger stake because we are carrying company-level risk.

Then make the immediate decision smaller:

  1. Confirm the relationship option.
  2. Confirm company/IP/account ownership cleanup.
  3. Authorize tranche one.
  4. Decide what must be true before tranche two voice starts.
  5. Decide what proof is needed before tranche three camp automation starts.
