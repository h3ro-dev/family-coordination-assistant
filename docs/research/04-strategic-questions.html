Strategic Questions To Ask

Priority 1: Foundation And Ownership

  1. What is Looheru.ai legally today: incorporated entity, project name, sole proprietorship, or unformed concept?
  2. Who owns the current codebase, domain, phone number, customer data, copy, and product concept?
  3. What was Barclay's role in the initial concept, and are there any written agreements, expectations, or contribution claims?
  4. Who else has contributed to strategy, design, engineering, prompt work, infrastructure, or user testing?
  5. Is there a cap table, advisor agreement, contractor agreement, or IP assignment already in place?
  6. Are there employer conflict, moonlighting, outside-business, or brand-use constraints from Intermountain?
  7. Can Tamara publicly use her Intermountain title in Looheru GTM, or should that remain background credibility only?

Priority 2: Customer And Problem Validation

  1. Who is the first customer segment: working physician mothers, all working moms, special-needs caregivers, high-income families, or healthcare employers?
  2. How many real target users have seen or used the product?
  3. What exact task caused users to say, "I would pay for this"?
  4. How often does the babysitter coordination problem occur per household per month?
  5. What do families do today: group text, spouse delegation, care.com, sittercity, calendar app, assistant, family member, or nothing?
  6. Is the pain strongest in finding sitters, contacting sitters, confirming logistics, remembering tasks, or reducing emotional load?
  7. What is the minimum repeat-use loop that happens weekly, not occasionally?
  8. What did the earlier physician-mother survey ask, how many people saw it, how many responded, and what patterns were strongest?
  9. Which users already pay for personal assistants, childcare coordination help, concierge services, or other substitutes?
  10. What exact coordination tasks consume the claimed 5-10 hours/week?

Priority 3: Product And Scope

  1. Is the product an assistant, concierge, workflow automation tool, caregiver network manager, or broader parent operating system?
  2. What is the exact boundary of what the assistant is allowed to do without parent approval?
  3. Does it only text saved contacts, or can it call, email, calendar-invite, or use voice agents?
  4. How are providers onboarded and consented before receiving automated messages?
  5. What happens when a sitter replies with ambiguity, conditions, price, or a question?
  6. What is the fallback when the AI misunderstands, contacts the wrong person, or confirms the wrong time?
  7. Does the parent see every outbound message before it is sent, or is the assistant autonomous?
  8. Is there human review today, and if yes, who provides it?
  9. What admin dashboard actions are required to support one family, ten families, and one hundred families?
  10. What data is stored about children, providers, schedules, health needs, and family preferences?

Priority 4: Pricing And Business Model

  1. What is the first paid offer and price?
  2. Is the business subscription-only, concierge-enhanced, usage-based, group coaching, certification, or a blend?
  3. What gross margin is expected after Twilio, AI, human review, support, and failed-task remediation?
  4. What usage cap prevents a heavy household from becoming unprofitable?
  5. Would the first segment pay $10/month, $25/month, $99/month, or only if bundled as an employer benefit?
  6. What does "group coaching" teach, and why is it attached to an execution product?
  7. What does "certification" certify: parents, caregivers, providers, or AI-assisted coordination practices?
  8. Is the economic buyer the parent, employer, association, clinic, school, or caregiver organization?
  9. What usage cap keeps a $20-$40/month subscription profitable?
  10. What is the estimated COGS per completed babysitter task, per summer-camp task, and per healthcare scheduling call?
  11. Which pricing model best matches user perception: subscription, task credits, concierge tier, or employer benefit?
  12. Would the first ICP rather pay for "time saved," "stress removed," "tasks completed," or "reliable family support"?
  13. What price does the target household compare this against: apps, personal assistants, childcare services, concierge medicine, or doing it themselves?

Priority 5: GTM

  1. What is Tamara's actual distribution channel: email list, LinkedIn following, speaking audience, physician mother community, private group, newsletter, or employer network?
  2. How many of the claimed 10,000 working physician mothers can she directly reach?
  3. What channel can convert this month without paid ads?
  4. What message resonates better: "coordination assistant," "AI family chief of staff," "babysitter coordinator," or "caregiver logistics assistant"?
  5. What proof will convince skeptical working mothers to trust the system with family logistics?
  6. What pilot promise is specific enough to sell but narrow enough to deliver?
  7. What is the referral loop after a successful booking?
  8. Are providers/caregivers annoyed by automated coordination, or do they find it useful?
  9. What does the founder-led first launch look like: LinkedIn, physician mother group, talk/keynote, private beta, or referral-only?
  10. What proof asset will convert: Tamara's story, demo video, before/after task completion, testimonials, or measurable hours saved?
  11. Can Tamara directly activate the physician-mother audience, or is it mostly brand adjacency?
  12. Which B2B buyer would care first: employer benefits, health-system workforce support, physician associations, or parent communities?

Priority 6: Healthcare And Compliance Boundary

  1. Will healthcare scheduling involve PHI, symptoms, diagnoses, insurance data, or provider instructions?
  2. Is Looheru intended to become HIPAA-covered, HIPAA-adjacent, or strictly nonclinical?
  3. Who is the user of record when an AI voice agent calls a clinic receptionist?
  4. Can the assistant represent itself as acting for the parent without creating consent or recording issues?
  5. What states/countries will it operate in, and what consent rules apply for recording, SMS, and AI voice calls?
  6. How will the product avoid medical advice, triage, or care-plan interpretation?
  7. If special-needs coordination becomes a wedge, what sensitive data protections are required before launch?
  8. Which first workflows keep Looheru clearly outside clinical care and employer-owned health-tech IP?
  9. What disclosure is required when an AI voice agent calls on behalf of a parent?
  10. Which launch states have AI voice, call recording, or automated-call rules that change product behavior?
  11. What must be deterministic rather than AI-driven to reduce legal/compliance risk?
  12. Can any data be anonymized for analytics or provider recommendations without damaging user trust?

Priority 7: Technical Diligence

  1. Where is the codebase, and who has admin access?
  2. What is the Railway production URL and deployment state?
  3. What database is used, and are backups, migrations, and environment separation configured?
  4. Is Twilio toll-free verification complete, pending, or rejected?
  5. Are Resend domain authentication and deliverability configured?
  6. What LLM provider/model is used, and what safeguards exist for tool calls and message sending?
  7. Are logs redacted for child names, phone numbers, and schedule details?
  8. How are secrets stored and rotated?
  9. What tests exist for the core coordination loop?
  10. What monitoring exists for failed outbound messages, delayed responses, and partial confirmations?

Priority 8: Partnership With Utlyze

  1. What does Tamara want from Utlyze: build partner, technical reviewer, venture studio, strategic advisor, GTM partner, or compliance architect?
  2. Is she looking for paid services, equity, rev-share, hybrid economics, or informal advice?
  3. What decision does she need after the first meeting?
  4. What work has already been done by others that Utlyze should not duplicate?
  5. What are the first 30-day outcomes she would consider a successful partnership?
  6. What is the minimum diligence Utlyze needs before touching production or accepting responsibility?
  7. Who owns product decisions if Utlyze disagrees with the current roadmap?
  8. What would make this a bad fit for either side?
  9. What ownership percentage, decision rights, and funding responsibility would feel fair if Utlyze becomes a build partner?
  10. Does Tamara want a slim solopreneur-style operating model, a venture studio model, or a services-led build model?
  11. What must be true before Tamara would accept equity dilution or shared ownership?
  12. What would Utlyze/SolutionStream be responsible for: architecture, build, GTM, funding, operations, support, compliance, or all of the above?
  13. What does "radical ownership" mean operationally when there is disagreement about product scope, risk, or monetization?

Recommended First Call Agenda

  1. Align on desired relationship: advice, due diligence, build partnership, or venture studio.
  2. Clarify entity, IP, contributors, and employer constraints before product planning.
  3. Review live prototype and architecture at a high level.
  4. Pick one wedge customer and one paid pilot offer.
  5. Define the next evidence packet: demo access, repo access, architecture diagram, customer interview notes, pilot list, and proposed economics.

Recommended May 21 Agenda

  1. Confirm the first ICP and first task loop.
  2. Review monetization propositions with pricing, usage caps, and COGS assumptions.
  3. Review partnership propositions with ownership, responsibility, funding, and decision-right tradeoffs.
  4. Decide the next build/test milestone and what must be learned from 10-25 pilot families.
  5. Identify legal/compliance research required before AI voice calls or healthcare scheduling.
  6. Agree on what can be public, what remains private, and what belongs in the public GitHub repo.
